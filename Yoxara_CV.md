2019-Present

Finding and building new solutions Beyond Standard Model with extensions from the fermionic and vectorial sector. Description: We will build and explore models that seek to explain dark matter, provide neutrinos with mass, and analyze the muon anomalous magnetic moment, lepton flavor violation, and collider physics, among other problems. In this sense, we will use computational tools used in high-energy particle physics, such as Mathematica, MadGraph5, SARAH, Feynrules, and CalcHep, to build and analyze the models phenomenologically.

2018-2019 Phenomenology of the type-II seesaw mechanism. Description: We reviewed the theoretical and experimental aspects of the type-Il seesaw model. This model is a mechanism that generates neutrino masses by adding a weak-scalar triplet to the Standard Model spectrum. This model is phenomenologically attractive because it can generate neutrino masses without invoking new physics effects at high energy scales. We revisited the constraints coming from the $\rho$-parameter and electroweak precision data, and we discussed collider signatures of the type II seesaw model focusing on the Large Hadron Collider. We exhibited the type-II seesaw model's viable parameter space and showed the complementarity between collider physics and lepton flavor violation. The project represented the first step toward our goal: to assess the High-Luminosity and High Energy LHC sensitivity to the type II seesaw model connected to lepton flavor violation and neutrino mass ordering (e-Print: 1909.07429 [hep-ph]).

2015-2016 Optical Properties of ZnO with Mn at 2\%. Description: Mn-doped ZnO ceramic pellets were sintered at two temperatures of $500^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$ and $900^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$. The materials' characterization was carried out by X-ray diffraction, scanning electron microscopy, optical absorption, photoluminescence, and FT-IR techniques.
